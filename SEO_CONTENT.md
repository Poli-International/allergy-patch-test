## What is the Allergy Patch Test Protocol Generator?

The Allergy Patch Test Protocol Generator is a free web tool that creates personalized 96-hour patch test protocols for body jewelry materials, tattoo inks, and topical products used in piercing and tattoo procedures. The tool generates step-by-step instructions based on three user inputs: the material being tested, the test site location, and the client's known skin sensitivity level.

Each generated protocol includes material-specific allergen information, preparation steps, a detailed 96-hour observation timeline with five checkpoints, and a reaction interpretation guide. The tool covers 11 material categories including surgical steel, implant-grade titanium, niobium, gold alloys, sterling silver, BioFlex polymer, PTFE/bioplast, black tattoo ink, color tattoo ink, and topical numbing creams.

## Who Should Use This Tool

**Tattoo and piercing studio owners** can implement the tool as part of their standard pre-procedure safety protocols. The generated protocols provide documentation for client records and demonstrate due diligence in allergy risk management.

**Professional piercers and tattoo artists** can use the tool to guide clients through material testing before procedures. The tool provides accurate, material-specific allergen information that artists can reference when discussing material options with clients.

**Clients with known skin sensitivities** can generate protocols to test materials before committing to a piercing or tattoo. The tool accounts for sensitive skin, eczema-prone skin, contact dermatitis history, and known metal allergies.

**Clients with history of contact dermatitis** receive extended observation guidance and warnings about false positive reactions. The tool recommends formal dermatology testing for clients with documented contact dermatitis.

**Studio managers implementing safety protocols** can use the tool to standardize patch testing procedures across their studio. The generated protocols provide consistent documentation for all material testing.

## How to Use the Allergy Patch Test Protocol Generator

### Step 1, Select the Material to Test

Open the "What to test" dropdown menu. Choose from three material categories:
- **Body jewelry metals**: Surgical steel, implant-grade titanium, niobium, 9ct gold, 18ct/14ct gold, sterling silver
- **Flexible/Polymer materials**: BioFlex polymer, PTFE/bioplast
- **Tattoo materials**: Black/greywash ink, color ink, topical numbing cream

Select the specific material you plan to use for the procedure.

### Step 2, Choose the Test Site

Open the "Test site" dropdown menu. Select from four locations:
- **Inner forearm** (recommended), most accessible and reliable
- **Behind ear**, discreet location
- **Inner wrist**, convenient for self-observation
- **Behind knee**, alternative if other sites are unavailable

The inner forearm is recommended for most tests due to easy access and reliable reaction reading.

### Step 3, Indicate Skin Sensitivity

Open the "Known skin sensitivity" dropdown menu. Select the option that matches the client's history:
- **Normal**, no known sensitivities
- **Sensitive skin / eczema-prone**
- **History of contact dermatitis**
- **Known metal allergy** (e.g., nickel)

The tool adjusts the protocol based on this selection, adding specific warnings and observation frequency recommendations.

### Step 4, Generate the Protocol

Click the "Generate Patch Test Protocol →" button. The tool produces a complete protocol including:
- Material-specific allergen information
- Preparation requirements
- 96-hour timeline with five checkpoints
- Reaction interpretation guide
- Instructions for positive test results

The protocol scrolls into view automatically. Review all sections with the client before beginning the test.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What materials can I test with this tool?

The tool supports 11 material categories: surgical steel, implant-grade titanium, niobium, 9ct gold, 18ct/14ct gold, sterling silver, BioFlex polymer, PTFE/bioplast, black/greywash tattoo ink, color tattoo ink, and topical numbing creams.

### 2. How long does the patch test take?

The complete protocol runs for 96 hours (4 days) with observation checkpoints at hours 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96. The 72-hour reading is considered the most diagnostically important for allergic contact dermatitis.

### 3. Can I test multiple materials at once?

The tool generates protocols for one material at a time. If testing multiple materials, generate separate protocols for each material and test them on different areas of the skin.

### 4. What does a positive reaction look like?

A positive reaction shows redness (erythema) plus papules (small raised bumps), vesicles (tiny fluid blisters), or infiltration (thickened skin) at the test site. These symptoms typically peak at 72 hours.

### 5. What should I do if the test is positive?

Do not proceed with the tested material. Document the reaction with photographs and consult a dermatologist. For jewelry, consider implant-grade titanium or BioFlex. For tattoo inks, request a different brand or formulation.

### 6. Does a negative patch test guarantee no reaction during the procedure?

No. Patch testing does not guarantee a procedure will be reaction-free. Some reactions only present at the full procedural dose. The tool includes this disclaimer in every generated protocol.

### 7. Can I use this tool for clients with a history of anaphylaxis?

The tool advises clients with a history of severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) to consult a dermatologist or allergist before any patch testing. The generated protocols are not a substitute for professional allergy testing.

### 8. What is the best test site location?

The inner forearm is recommended for most tests. It provides easy access for observation, reliable reaction reading, and is less visible than other locations.

### 9. How does the tool handle different skin sensitivity levels?

The tool adjusts the protocol based on the selected sensitivity level. For sensitive skin, it recommends 4-hour checks during the first 24 hours. For contact dermatitis history, it warns about false positive reactions. For known metal allergies, it suggests alternative materials.

### 10. Is the tool suitable for testing numbing creams?

Yes. The tool includes topical numbing cream (lidocaine) as a test material. The protocol specifies applying the cream under occlusion (covered with cling film and tape) and warns that the cream base ingredients are often the allergen rather than the anesthetic itself.
